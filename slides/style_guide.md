# Slide style guide

Extracted 2026-09-02 from three real sources — `DowlingLab_template.pptx` (a 2018 conference template, 2 slides), `Dowling Teaching Controls MPC Academy 2025.pptx` (a real, delivered 26-slide talk, MPC Academy, Wayne State, August 2025), and `2026 May Seminar Optimizing Experiments.pdf` (a real, delivered 51-slide faculty seminar, May 2026) — plus Alex's direct critique of this deck's stale draft sections. The two PowerPoint files live in the lab's Google Drive (Conferences and Presentations), the PDF in Group Papers; none are in this repo. The analysis (word counts, font sizes, positions) was done directly against the pptx XML and, for the PDF, against extracted text and rendered page images. This file is the rule set the Beamer deck (`main.tex`, `sections/*.tex`) must follow; it is not itself a PowerPoint or PDF document.

## What the audit found

**Text density.** Word count per slide across the 26 real talk slides ranged from 6 to 229, but the distribution is bimodal, not a spread: most slides (18 of 26) run under 45 words of prose and many under 20 — these are the image-forward slides that carry the talk. A handful of outliers (the agenda slide, two "why this works" summary slides, and one 229-word reference/citation slide) are backup or reference material, not narrative slides, and read that way — dense text is acceptable there because the audience isn't meant to read it live, only to have it available. **Rule: narrative content slides stay under ~20–25 words of body prose** (titles and diagram labels don't count). If a slide needs more, split it or demote the extra material to a backup slide.

The May 2026 faculty seminar (a different genre — a technical research talk to peers who are expected to read equations and tables, not just listen) confirms the *backup-slide* half of this pattern independently (its own densest pages, 172–287 words, are quad-box definition grids and literature-citation blocks — the same role the MPC talk's 229-word page plays) but runs denser throughout even on its narrative slides, in full-bleed multi-column layouts. That's a genre difference, not a contradiction: a technical seminar to specialists reads differently from a teaching talk to a mixed room. **This deck follows the MPC talk's sparser register** — the CBE seminar audience is meant to listen to a spoken narrative, the same job that talk was doing.

**Font sizes.** The real deck's own slide master defines exactly one title size (32pt, bold) and a two-level body hierarchy (28pt / 24pt) — that discipline is already there in the master, even though individual slides drift from it ad hoc. This repo's own draft sections had drifted further: `\Large`, `\large`, `\small`, and `\scriptsize` were all in use as body text with no rule distinguishing them. **Rule, effective now: exactly two body-text sizes.** `\large` for the one big idea a slide argues (the NDBlue bold callout pattern already used throughout — see `\slidepoint` below); `\small` for everything supporting it — captions, attributions, footers, secondary explanation. `\scriptsize`/`\tiny` are not body text; they're reserved for diagram micro-labels and code (`lstlisting` already sets `\scriptsize`), exactly the way the real deck reserves 10–12pt Courier New for citation lines and URLs, never for the point being made.

**Color.** The template's own theme XML carries no real brand palette — it's the default Office color scheme (`4472C4` blue, etc.); whatever ND identity the real deck has comes from images (the logo) and a few manually-applied text colors, not a defined scheme. That confirms the right move already made in `main.tex`: define the actual official ND colors once (`NDBlue` `#0C2340`, `Dome Gold` `#C99700`, `Irish Green` `#00843D`, documented with WCAG contrast in `theme/README.md`) and use only those plus the Okabe-Ito data palette — never the incidental blues that show up in pasted clip-art (`0022F5`, `1511EE` were leftover artifact colors in the real deck, not deliberate choices).

**Logo placement.** The real deck's slide master places the ND wordmark in the **bottom-left corner** — roughly 1% of slide width from the left edge, 1% from the bottom, sized to about 10% of slide width. This is Alex's own established convention, not a new stylistic add-on. `main.tex` now reproduces it: a small `figures/nd_logo.png` renders bottom-left on every content frame via a background template (title slide excepted — it already carries its own full-size logo in the closing columns, and a second small copy there would collide).

**Layout.** Every real content slide keeps title, body, and page number in the same three fixed positions (title top-left in a full-width bar, page number bottom-right, content filling the middle) — nothing floats between slides. The Beamer deck already has this from `\frametitle`/`footerSimple`; no change needed there.

**Whitespace and grouping (from the May 2026 seminar).** Three patterns worth carrying into content-drafting, all confirmed real and repeated (not one-offs):

- **Colored outline boxes group parallel content into panels** — three research pillars, each in its own rounded-rectangle border (blue/green/orange), side by side. This is functional grouping, not decoration — the box is standing in for a table's cell borders. It's a different thing from the "decorative accent stripe" anti-pattern (a line with no content-structuring job); use it for genuinely parallel, comparable content (the Act II regression-harness before/after, a claim-tracing table) rather than reaching for it by default.
- **A long necessary quote gets selective bold/color emphasis on its key phrases, not a summary.** The exact same digital-twin definition paragraph appears verbatim in both the MPC talk and the May seminar; each time, a handful of phrases are bolded and colored so the paragraph can be skimmed without being cut. This is the same move `\keyterm` already makes in this deck — direct confirmation it's a real habit, not just a Beamer nicety, and license to use it on other necessary-but-long text (a definition, a quoted commit message) instead of paraphrasing it shorter.
- **Equation terms are annotated in place**, with each term in a sum or expression colored and connected by a bracket to its plain-language meaning directly below it, rather than a separate legend or caption. Available as a technique for this deck's own equations/tables (e.g., the claim-tracing table) if one ever needs term-by-term annotation, though nothing currently in the storyboard requires it.

**Not carried over: photo-credit headshots.** The seminar routinely places a small square headshot in a slide's corner to credit the specific student or collaborator who did that work — a normal, low-key convention in Alex's own research talks. This deck doesn't use it: the seminar's own naming convention (see `../notes/demo_ideas.md`) keeps every non-public example generic specifically to avoid identifying students, and a headshot would defeat that regardless of whether a name is spoken.

## Rules for this deck going forward

1. **One title size.** The theme's `frametitle` template already enforces this everywhere except the cover (title) slide, which is its own one-off typographic treatment (a cover, not a body slide) and is exempt.
2. **Two body sizes, no more.** `\large` for the slide's one argument; `\small` for support. Never `\Large`, `\normalsize` mixed in ad hoc, or `\footnotesize` as body text.
3. **Logo bottom-left**, automatic via the background template — section files don't need to add it themselves. Sized larger than the audit match (Alex's call, 2026-09-02: ~150% of the ~10%-of-width the real deck used) — re-check any new content-heavy or fully-centered frame visually, since the bigger logo collides more easily with a bottom line of body text than the original size did.
4. **Colors**: `NDBlue` or plain black for text — never `Muted` gray on body prose, captions, or asides (Alex's call, 2026-09-02: gray read as too low-contrast). `NDGold` and `IrishGreen` are for accents only (rules, bullets, icons), the same restriction as before. `Muted` gray survives only for non-text UI elements that are deliberately meant to look inactive/de-emphasized — the act-strip's unlit stage boxes and connecting arrows — and for code-comment styling inside `lstlisting`, both a different case from a sentence someone has to read.
5. **No closing takeaway bar.** The gold-rule `\takeaway{}` device is retired (Alex's call, 2026-09-02: it mostly repeated the frame's title in different words) and removed from `main.tex` entirely — no callers remain anywhere in the deck. State the slide's point in its title instead; treat the body — table, list, quote — as evidence for a claim already made, not build-up toward one revealed at the bottom.
6. **Text density**: ~20–25 words of prose per narrative slide, matching the MPC talk's sparser register (this deck's genre), not the denser full-bleed technical-seminar register. If a slide needs more, it's doing two jobs — split it, or make it a labeled backup slide if the extra density is reference material, not narrative.
7. **Grouping boxes are for genuinely parallel content**, not decoration — three colored-outline panels for three comparable things, never a lone accent stripe.
8. **Balance vertical whitespace — center, don't pin to the top.** Wrap a frame's content in `\vfill ... \vfill` (the norm elsewhere in this deck), and for side-by-side content use `\begin{columns}[c]`, never `[t]` — `[t]` top-aligns each column independently, which looks fine when both columns happen to be the same height and leaves a ragged strip of empty space below the shorter one when they aren't. Caught 2026-09-03: the roadmap slide's five-item list sat top-aligned next to a taller image with no closing `\vfill`, reading as "ugly whitespace on the right" — the fix was `[t]` → `[c]` plus `\vfill` above and below, not touching the column widths. **When a slide looks top-heavy or lopsided, check alignment and `\vfill` placement before touching content or proportions.**
9. **Every visual element has to earn its space, not just be available.** Before adding a diagram, icon, or repeated motif to a slide, ask what it changes about what the audience understands. If the honest answer is "nothing, it's just consistent with other slides" or "it repeats what the title/table already says," cut it — don't add it, and don't keep it out of habit once it's built. Caught 2026-09-03: the roadmap slide's bottom act-strip (all five stages, none lit) re-stated the same outline the slide's own table had just shown, added nothing new, and at the enlarged logo size (rule 3) collided with the corner logo — removed rather than repositioned. The act-strip earns its place on the `\stagedivider` transition slides (where one stage lights up — new information) and on slides mid-argument that need the orientation; a second, redundant copy on the same slide as the outline it's illustrating does not.
10. **Before calling a slide done, look at it rendered, not just at the source.** A frame can compile clean and still be visually wrong — lopsided whitespace, a collision with the corner logo, a decorative element with no job. Render to an image (`pdftoppm`) and actually look, per-slide, for any frame touched that session; don't infer correctness from "it compiled."
11. **Fixing a corner-logo collision: use a fixed minimum, not a bigger `\vfill` weight.** Discovered 2026-09-03, the hard way, after the logo grew again and moved tighter into the corner (rule 3): on a content-dense frame where the trailing `\vfill` is already collapsed near zero, changing it to `\vspace{0pt plus Nfill}` for a larger `N` does almost nothing — there's no leftover stretch to redistribute, so the ratio between glue instances is close to irrelevant. What actually works is a **fixed floor**: `\vspace{1.8cm plus 1fill}` as the frame's final glue (1.8cm comfortably clears the logo's footprint at its current size/position; recompute if either changes again — logo height ≈ its set width × 0.4125, the image's own aspect ratio). If a frame still collides after that, the fix is reducing actual content height (smaller font on a code block via `\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\tiny]`, a shorter image height, tighter inter-paragraph spacing, or trimmed wording) — not more glue tuning.
